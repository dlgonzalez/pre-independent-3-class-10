Space Exploration & Passengers

Speaking Listening Vocabulary Film Work
Opening

Student oral presentations ~25 min

You prepared a short presentation on a space topic — either a summary of a TED-Ed video or a topic you investigated independently. Now it's time to share it.

Presentations
  • Deliver your summary to the class. Aim for 1–2 minutes.
  • After each presentation, at least one classmate asks a follow-up question.
  • Try to connect ideas across presentations — did anyone choose a similar topic? Did you learn anything surprising?
Reference resource: TED-Ed Space Lessons ↗
Speaking tip
You don't need to memorize your presentation. Use notes, but try to look up and speak to your classmates rather than reading word for word. Use signal phrases like "What I found most interesting was…" or "Something that surprised me was…"

Listening

Introduction to Passengers ~20 min

Passengers (2016) is a science fiction film set aboard a spacecraft called the Avalon, carrying 5,000 colonists on a 120-year journey to a distant planet called Homestead II. The passengers are in hibernation pods — but Jim wakes up 90 years too early.

Watch & work — Jim wakes up

Watch the first clip together and complete the activities on the page. You will practise:

  • Listening for gist and specific information
  • Vocabulary in context
  • Comprehension questions
Activity page: listeninenglish.com — Jim Wakes Up ↗
Before you watch
Think about this: Jim wakes up alone on a spaceship with 90 years left in the journey. No one else is awake. What would you do in his situation? Share your ideas quickly with a partner.
Independent work

Choose your clip — individual film work ~20 min

Each of you selects a different section of Passengers to work on independently. Use the listeninenglish.com format as your guide: watch your clip, complete the activities, and take notes for your summary.

Your task
  1. Choose a clip from the film (your teacher will assign sections to avoid overlap).
  2. Watch your section and work through the exercises on the page.
  3. Take notes: What happens? Who is involved? What changes?
  4. Prepare a 1–2 minute spoken summary to share with the class.
How to structure your summary
  • Setting the scene: Where are we? What has happened so far?
  • Main event: What happens in your section?
  • Reaction / consequence: How do the characters respond? What does it mean for the story?
Closing

Putting the film together ~15 min

Each of you shares your section summary in order. Together, you will reconstruct the whole film — as if you're building a puzzle.

Class reconstruction
  • Listen carefully to your classmates' summaries — you may hear details you missed.
  • After all summaries, discuss: Did the film follow the direction you expected? Were there any surprises?
  • Final question: Would you recommend Passengers? Use one of today's idioms in your answer.
